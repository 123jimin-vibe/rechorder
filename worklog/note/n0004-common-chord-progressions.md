## Principles

- Research background for suggestion heuristics; implemented behavior is governed by s0003 and s0005. Roman numerals are relative to the tonic; lowercase is minor, ° diminished, ♭/♯ chromatic roots.
- Every genre reuses a small set of moves: fifth motion, stepwise roots, third-related roots, stepwise bass lines, borrowed color, and applied dominants. A useful heuristic rewards these moves and functions rather than one genre's favorite loop.
- Chord density is a style trait: pop mostly uses triads, jazz mostly sevenths. Neighbors set the expected density.

## Progressions by tradition

### Pop, rock, folk, country

- I–V–vi–IV and its rotations vi–IV–I–V, IV–I–V–vi ("axis" loops); I–vi–IV–V (doo-wop); I–IV–V; I–V–IV; I–IV–vi–V; I–iii–IV–V.
- I–♭VII–IV (mixolydian rock); ♭VII–IV–I (double plagal); I–V–♭VII–IV; i–♭VII–♭VI–♭VII; i–♭VI–♭III–♭VII (minor "axis").
- Blues: I7–IV7–I7–V7–IV7–I7 over twelve bars; every chord may be a dominant seventh.
- Mixture cadences: IV–iv–I; ♭VI–♭VII–I; I–♭VI–I.

### Jazz and bossa nova

- ii–V–I in major, iiø–V7–i in minor; I–vi–ii–V turnaround; iii–vi–ii–V; I–VI7–ii–V (applied VI7).
- Circle of fifths: I–IV–vii°–iii–vi–ii–V–I; vi–ii–V–I–IV–vii°–III7–vi ("Autumn Leaves" / "Fly Me to the Moon").
- Substitutions: ♭II7 for V7 (tritone), iv–♭VII7–I (backdoor), chromatic mediants by major thirds (Coltrane), Imaj7–II7–ii7–♭II7 (Ipanema).

### Classical and hymn

- I–IV–V–I, I–ii6–V–I, I–vi–IV–ii–V–I; deceptive V–vi; half cadence to V; Picardy third in minor.
- Applied dominants V/x and vii°/x for any diatonic goal; Neapolitan ♭II6–V; augmented sixth chords to V.
- Passing and cadential inversions: I–V6–vi, I–I6–IV, I–V64–I6, I64–V. Pachelbel: I–V–vi–iii–IV–I–IV–V with a stepwise bass when inverted (I–V6–vi–iii6–IV–I6–IV–V).

### Descending bass lines

- I–V6–vi–(I64 or vi/♭7)–IV–I6–ii–V: the "Whiter Shade of Pale" / "Piano Man" line, bass 1–7–6–5–4–3–2–5.
- Andalusian i–♭VII–♭VI–V (bass 1–♭7–♭6–5); chromatic passing I–I7/♭7–IV6 (bass 1–♭7–6) and I–♯i°7–ii (bass 1–♯1–2).
- Stepwise bass explains most inversions; once a bass line has started, continuing it by step matters more than root function.

### Gospel, R&B, neo-soul

- I–iii–IV–V, I–vi–ii–V with extensions; chromatic passing diminished I–♯i°–ii–♯ii°–iii; IV–iv–I; ♭VI–♭VII–I; V/ii–ii–V–I; sevenths, ninths and sus chords as color rather than function.

### Film, game, J-pop, EDM

- Chromatic mediants: I–♭VI, I–♭III, I–III, i–♭VI–i; Lydian I–II; ♭VI–♭VII–I ("Mario cadence").
- IV–V–iii–vi ("royal road", often IVmaj7–V7–iii7–vi7); vi–IV–V–I; i–VI–III–VII and i–VII–VI–VII loops.

## Consequences for heuristics

1. Root motion frequency: down a fifth (strongest, dominant and circle), up a fifth (I–V, IV–I), whole step (IV–V, V–vi, ♭VI–♭VII), thirds (I–vi, IV–vi, vi–IV, chromatic mediants), semitone steps and same-root quality changes (I–I7, IV–iv, V–v) are common; tritone roots are rare.
2. Function frequency in major: I, V, IV, vi, ii are ubiquitous; iii and vii° are secondary; applied dominants, ♭VII, iv, ♭VI, ♭III and ♭II are color. In minor: i, V or v, iv, VI, VII, III lead; ii° is secondary.
3. Phrase behavior: chords rarely repeat immediately; returning to a chord heard two chords ago is common only as I–V–I or I–IV–I.
4. Inversions almost always serve a stepwise bass; slash chords should be recommended when they continue such a line and otherwise stay in root position.
5. Voice-leading smoothness explains many chromatic choices (iv, V/vi, ♯i°7); a small total movement is evidence of coherence even when the chord is outside the key.
